openFerment
A white paper on an open engineering platform
October 22 2026
Version 0.3
Overview
openFerment is a proposed open-source engineering platform for work people can inspect, test, build, 
operate, and improve. Its first flagship is cellulosic ethanol from characterized biomass, with batch 
conversion and explicitly described recovery. Its wider purpose is transferable productive knowledge 
across manufacturing disciplines.
Postdoc is an AI research coordinator directed by the user. It uses a hosted API, installed tools, and 
curated, versioned evidence. Independent internet research is disabled by default; user-requested 
acquisition uses a controlled import. The user sets goals and limits. Postdoc plans, delegates, 
calculates, proposes ideas, and explains results.
Lasting records distinguish measurements, quotations, calculations, estimates, and assumptions. 
Uncertainty may use justified distributions, bounds, alternative explanations, or unquantified 
unknowns. Postdoc may estimate and generate ideas without a traceable publication, preserving their 
generated origin and limits. Ideas are hypotheses to examine.
The intended advantage is testable: can this environment help an equally capable AI produce more 
dependable answers with less measured work? Compare the simplest useful workflow against a 
competent general agent. Specialist coordination must earn its cost.
The scope includes the machines that make the product: editable designs, fabrication, controls, 
commissioning, cleaning, maintenance, and operating experience. Another team needs both the 
design and practical knowledge to reproduce production.
Projects may be private, shared, or published. Public contributions need usable rights to study, 
modify, reproduce, manufacture, and sell the work. Commercial participation belongs in a platform 
independent of any one business.1
A nonprofit steward and sustainable budget are proposed to maintain the platform, hold entrusted 
assets, and support its public terms.
Status of this paper The repository contains a prototype. The connected platform and flagship 
integration remain proposals. Section 13 distinguishes inspected code from demonstrated behavior. 
Sections 16 and 17 define the flagship and comparative evaluation. April 2027 is an internal handoff 
target, not public release or a promise of a complete optimized ethanol plant.
1 The founder intends to establish a company in Rwanda that uses openFerment and publishes its own technical work. Its 
product is undecided. It would be one commercial example of the approach, with the same public rights as other users. The 
platform and its public purpose should remain independent of that business.

1 Purpose and scope
Make productive knowledge transferable
openFerment aims to increase people's ability to make useful things. A good result may be a better 
experiment, a working machine, a more reliable process, a trained operator, or a production method 
another team can reproduce.
Public information can still be difficult to use. A paper may omit a detail needed to repeat an 
experiment. A simulation may hide an important assumption. A drawing may leave out how a 
fabricator solved a difficult joint. A successful batch may never return to the model that predicted it. 
openFerment should preserve the relationships that make these pieces understandable together.
The economic aim is to create useful wealth and share the means to create it. People can earn 
money by producing goods, building machines, and providing skilled services using public knowledge. 
Openness should make independent work possible, including commercial competition. A useful 
platform does not need to own every activity it helps create.
Start with bioprocess engineering
The initial domain is the use of organisms, cells, or biological materials to make products. The first 
reference case is cellulosic ethanol, starting with one documented corn-stover process and a declared 
batch-conversion boundary. Switchgrass is a candidate extension after its evidence and conditions 
are checked. Neither this choice nor the broader architecture establishes support for arbitrary 
feedstocks.
Much of the supporting work is general: managing projects, tracing inputs, recording predictions, 
organizing agents, identifying machines, and preserving results. These shared capabilities can 
support chemical processing and other manufacturing. Each new domain still needs appropriate 
models, tools, terminology, evaluations, and people who understand its limits.
The platform should integrate established scientific and engineering software where suitable. It should 
not attempt to replace every specialized design package, instrument system, or production 
application. Its responsibility is to connect the work and preserve enough context to inspect and 
repeat it.
2 The lab model
The user directs the work
The useful part of the lab metaphor is the division of responsibility. The user acts as project lead, like 
a principal investigator setting a lab's direction. They define the question, constraints, budget, and 
decision to be made. Postdoc coordinates the work within that authority.
Calling the user the principal investigator does not grant a professional qualification or authority over 
other people. A real organization may divide responsibility among a project owner, spending approver, 
qualified engineer, operator, reviewer, and publication approver. openFerment should support that 
division explicitly.
A user should be able to delegate routine work without approving every step. Searching within an 
agreed budget, running an allowed simulation, and preparing a draft can have standing permission.

Changing an authoritative record, spending beyond a limit, releasing a public report, and operating 
equipment need their own permissions. Approval should fit the action and its consequences.
Postdoc coordinates the research
Postdoc is the main point of contact for the project. It should understand the question, find what is 
already known, identify missing information, choose useful analyses, assign specialist work, and 
report what follows from the results.
The name describes a working relationship. A researcher assists a project lead by doing research and 
exercising judgment within a defined role. The software borrows that relationship without claiming to 
be a trained human, to possess professional authority, or to be reliable simply because of its name.
The Home page should therefore open into a conversation with Postdoc in a chosen project. 
Alongside the conversation, users should be able to see the plan, current tasks, spending, important 
assumptions, pending decisions, and results. They should be able to open the underlying sources, 
designs, and runs directly. Conversation is the entry point; saved project records hold the work.
Specialists and task workers do bounded jobs
Specialist agents can handle retrieval from the approved library, metabolic modeling, kinetics, 
recovery, equipment, and costing. Smaller task workers can extract a table, match a quotation, 
normalize units, or inspect a file. A worker may be an AI model or ordinary software. In the flagship, 
workers have installed tools and approved evidence; they do not acquire independent browsing 
authority.
The metaphor of graduate students and technicians helps explain delegation. It does not require a 
fixed model hierarchy. A simple calculation should usually use a checked calculation tool. A difficult 
research decision may need a capable model and independent review. Fine-tuned models are an 
option when evidence shows that they improve a task.
Postdoc can distribute work across many instances. Continuity must therefore live in shared records: 
the notebook, task history, sources, decisions, versions, and calibration results. The platform should 
retain what is needed to explain the work, with appropriate privacy and retention rules. A long chat 
transcript alone is an unreliable lab memory.
3 One complete investigation
Move from a question to a testable decision
A first investigation asks: given this characterized corn-stover lot and a supported conversion route, 
which assumptions most affect ethanol output, what limited alternatives can be compared, and what 
should be measured next? It uses a batch conversion model and declares the operating mode of each 
connected stage. A historical reference process is preserved separately from any modified candidate.
Postdoc should clarify the intended product, conditions, scale, available resources, and decision. It 
should then propose a plan that the user can understand and change. The plan may need literature 
research, biological constraints, a process model, a recovery train, an equipment design, and a 
preliminary cost estimate.

The workflow is connected but can branch and return to earlier questions:
1. Define the goal, constraints, permissions, budget, and criteria for a useful answer.
2. Gather relevant sources and existing project work, keeping their origins and reuse rights.
3. Identify inputs, missing information, estimates, and assumptions.
4. Compare process options using suitable models and specialist tools.
5. Connect a selected option to equipment requirements, operating conditions, and economics.
6. Record predictions and prepare a versioned procedure for a real test.
7. Perform the work with appropriate people and capture the actual conditions and results.
8. Compare predictions with outcomes and explain what the test supports.
9. Revise the relevant records and prepare a deliberate, permitted release of useful work.
Research need not always end in a physical run. A literature review, a failed feasibility calculation, or 
a useful equipment comparison can be a complete result for a particular question. The record should 
explain what was established and what remains untested.
Keep the whole investigation understandable
A Study is the proposed record of an investigation, linking its question, sources, input choices, 
calculations, designs, predictions, and results. A project may contain many Studies. A process design 
may be tested in several runs. A physical machine may serve several projects over time.
Suppose a growth model predicts the time needed to reach a target concentration. A vessel 
calculation then uses that result, and a recovery model uses the expected harvest stream. The 
economic estimate depends on all three. The Study should preserve those connections, the units and 
assumptions at each transfer, and the versions used.
If a later experiment shows a different growth rate, the platform should identify which later results may 
need review. It should preserve the old results rather than silently rewriting the history. Re-running 
selected work can then produce a new, linked version.
4 The lasting project record
Store work as more than conversation
Postdoc needs records it can retrieve and check. The user needs records they can inspect, export, 
and hand to another team. The core record types should have clear identities and relationships.

People, organizations, materials, samples, costs, and tasks also need identities where they affect 
interpretation. A sample should be traceable to the run and collection conditions that produced it. A 
calculation should identify the material or stream it represents.
The final schema should be developed against actual workflows. These responsibilities do not imply 
that every concept needs a separate service or database.
Preserve versions and useful lineage
An important result should explain which inputs and methods produced it. A changed value, design, 
procedure, or interpretation should create an attributable revision. Raw observations and later 
corrections should remain distinguishable.
Content hashes can identify exact files and results. They do not prove that the contents are true. A 
replay can help test whether a calculation is repeatable under its saved conditions. Some software, 
model services, or hardware cannot be reproduced exactly; the record should state those limits.
An amendment should not erase the original observation. Equally, a responsible record system needs 
retention, privacy, and deletion policies. Traceability does not require publishing personal information 
or retaining every file forever.
Preserve connections between tools
A result passed between modules needs a defined meaning. Units, composition, time basis, scale, 
and process conditions must agree. A stream should identify what flows, how much flows, and the 
basis of that amount.
The Study should identify which calculation is responsible for each material or energy balance. 
Transfers should expose losses, missing components, and unsupported conversions. A numerical 
result is not reliable merely because every individual tool ran successfully.

5 Evidence estimates and uncertainty
Distinguish origin from applicability
Origin explains how information entered the project. It may be quoted from a source, measured in a 
run, calculated by software, estimated by a model, assumed by a person, or adopted as a convention. 
A supplier specification is another useful source with its own conditions.
Applicability asks whether that information fits the present question. In bioprocess work, strain, 
medium, temperature, operating mode, equipment, scale, and measurement method can matter. A 
genuine measurement can be unsuitable for another system. A literature value can be useful when its 
transfer is explained.
When information is transferred between conditions, the platform should record the reasoning, 
method, and additional uncertainty. Origin, applicability, review, and permission are separate 
properties. They should not be collapsed into a single status such as trusted.
Allow estimates with a clear basis
Postdoc should be able to offer a preliminary estimate when that helps a decision. It should identify 
the estimate, explain the basis available, give relevant conditions, and state how it could be checked. 
It should not invent a citation or imply that a remembered pattern is a measured result.
A useful answer can be a range, an order of magnitude, or a statement that the available information 
does not support an estimate. Numerical uncertainty needs a basis too. An invented confidence 
interval can mislead as much as an invented exact value.
An AI estimate of a growth parameter may help plan an experiment. It should remain an estimate 
while the project uses it. If a later run measures that parameter, the measurement becomes a new 
linked record. If the run measures only final output, it may test the overall prediction without identifying 
that parameter separately.
Numeric validation should preserve origin and limitations, and match the evidence to the decision. A 
blanket ban on estimates would prevent useful preliminary work. Exact validation rules need task-
specific design and evaluation.
Make confidence inspectable
Confidence may depend on source quality, measurement quality, applicability, independent 
agreement, review, and past predictive performance. These components can have different methods 
and meanings. A score is useful only when its interpretation and limits are clear.
Several models agreeing is weak evidence if they repeat the same source or share the same failure. 
Several outputs derived from one measurement are not several independent measurements. The 
platform should preserve these dependencies where they matter.
Some decisions require a combined ranking. The method should be explicit and tested against the 
actual decision. There is no reason to impose one universal confidence score across literature 
extraction, equipment readiness, biological prediction, and legal review.

Carry uncertainty into the result
Important uncertainty should survive calculations, charts, exports, and reports. Where the available 
information supports it, tools can examine ranges, sensitivity, and alternative scenarios. Correlated 
inputs should not be treated as independent without justification.
Sensitivity analysis asks which uncertain inputs most affect the answer. It can help Postdoc 
recommend the next measurement or experiment. The best next test may be the one that changes a 
decision, rather than the one that produces the largest amount of data.
Represent incomplete knowledge honestly
For openFerment, uncertainty is the structured record of incomplete knowledge, genuine variability, or 
unresolved alternatives affecting a claim, model, prediction, hypothesis, or decision. The record 
explains its origin, evidence, applicable conditions, representation, dependencies, and possible 
consequences. Quantification is not compulsory when it has no defensible basis.
Useful distinctions include measurement limitations, variation between material lots, uncertain 
parameters, alternative model structures, extrapolation, unknown mechanisms, and uncertainty about 
which decision to take. These are overlapping descriptions of a problem, not seven independent 
random variables or a universal ranking of evidence.

Measurement uncertainty needs its method and interpretation. A reported plus-or-minus range should 
say what it represents. NIST's Type A and Type B distinguish methods of evaluating measurement 
uncertainty; they are not names for material variability and missing scientific knowledge. The broader 
project record also needs model and decision limits beyond measurement uncertainty. [10]
An absent contaminant measurement is not evidence of no contaminant effect. A bounded scenario is 
not automatically a probability interval. Biomass composition fractions and other dependent inputs 
must respect their shared basis and constraints; independently sampling all fractions can create 
impossible material. A narrow calculated interval remains conditional on the chosen model and what it 
leaves out.
Begin with a useful ledger of origins, missing information, conditions and alternative explanations. Add 
propagation where the inputs justify it. Later, compare predictions with appropriate observations and 
evaluate interval behavior across relevant cases. A single favorable result does not establish 
calibration. Formal value-of-information calculations require justified probability and decision models; 
a plainly reasoned recommendation for a discriminating measurement can be useful before those 
exist.

6 Postdoc agents and model routing
Give Postdoc a clear working method
Postdoc should begin with the user's goal and project record. It should identify what can be answered 
from existing evidence, which tasks need tools, and where the user must decide. Its plan should 
remain visible and revisable.
Each delegated task needs enough context to be meaningful: the question, relevant inputs, expected 
result, allowed tools, budget, time limit, privacy constraints, and action permissions. The returned 
work should identify sources, computed results, assumptions, uncertainty, failures, and cost as 
applicable.
Postdoc reviews the returned work for relevance, consistency, and missing support. It may ask for 
corrections or an independent check. Review by another AI is useful only to the extent that it adds 
effective scrutiny. It does not establish truth by itself.
The final response should connect conclusions to the underlying records and explain what action they 
support. A concise answer can sit above a detailed record without losing the evidence needed to 
inspect it.
Make scientific variation a deliberate capability
Postdoc should have a bounded variation role: propose alternatives, use analogies, question 
assumptions, and offer competing explanations when the installed evidence is incomplete. Its 
pretrained associations can inspire an idea even when no particular training source can be identified. 
Numerical estimates remain allowed with their generated origin, basis and limitations visible. Missing 
citations must not be repaired by inventing a source.
Creative sampling and physical uncertainty have different meanings. The probability of generating a 
sentence is not the probability that its proposed mechanism is true. More randomness does not 
establish more useful or novel science. Evaluate whether ideas are distinct, physically plausible, 
relevant to the decision, and capable of being tested.
A hypothesis record should preserve the proposal, generating model and relevant context, cited 
support where available, extrapolations, assumptions, proposed mechanism, necessary tests, and 
evaluation history. Keep the generated text and actual evaluation results; do not use hidden model 
reasoning as experimental evidence. A seed may help replay, but hosted models need not reproduce 
an identical idea.
An idea can be retained, screened, translated into a supported candidate, evaluated conditionally, 
tested physically later, or rejected with a reason. A proposal requiring a new scientific model stays in 
the hypothesis collection until that model exists. It must not enter the evaluated design ranking 
through invented response coefficients. Screening should preserve unconventional ideas without 
granting them the status of checked designs.
Route tasks using measured performance
Routing means choosing the model or software worker, effort, tools, budget, timeout, and fallback for 
a job. It belongs in the initial architecture, alongside the evaluations needed to guide it.
Postdoc needs sufficient capability for planning and reviewing difficult work. That does not imply one 
permanently assigned model. A specialist can use a different model when it performs the task better.

Small workers can handle narrow, well-tested jobs cheaply. Ordinary code should perform defined 
calculations and transformations where it is suitable.
The choice should account for task quality, cost, latency, available context, privacy, tool behavior, and 
failure rate. A cheap answer that needs repeated repair may cost more overall. An expensive answer 
still needs evaluation.
Start with a small number of useful task families and tested routes. Expand to many simultaneous 
agents when the work and measured benefits justify it. Hundreds of workers are a capacity ambition, 
not a requirement for every question. Unbounded delegation could spend money without resolving the 
project.
Evaluate the router as well as the models
Evaluation sets should represent the work the platform actually performs. They can test extraction 
against source text, calculations against suitable references, retrieval against useful evidence, and 
recommendations against stated criteria. Independent test cases should remain separate from 
examples used to tune a route or model.
Record model and tool versions, task conditions, quality, cost, time, and failure modes. Compare the 
complete route, including checking and retries, with simpler alternatives. The evaluation should also 
test when the system needs to abstain or ask for help.
Version changes can alter performance. Important routes need periodic checks and explicit fallback 
behavior. The router should be able to stop when the remaining budget or evidence cannot support a 
useful result.
Support providers without promising universal interchangeability
A common interface should support suitable commercial providers and local models. It needs to 
handle differences in tool use, output formats, context, usage reporting, errors, and cancellation. 
Provider-specific features may still require adapters and tests.
Users should know which provider receives their project information. Credentials, requester identity, 
reserved and settled spending, and the account responsible for payment need appropriate protection 
and records. Access to a powerful model must not silently grant it wider permissions.
Treat memory as a maintained record
Postdoc should remember goals, decisions, unresolved questions, important results, and why earlier 
choices were made. Memory should point to inspectable evidence and versions. Old or conflicting 
information needs review rather than automatic reuse.
This continuity belongs to the project and its authorized users. Separate private projects must not leak 
into one another through retrieval, shared caches, summaries, or model training. Team disagreement 
should remain attributable instead of being flattened into a false consensus.
Retrieved documents, files, and tool responses are material to analyze. Their text cannot grant new 
authority to the system. Permissions should be enforced by the application and tools, with an audit 
trail for important actions.

7 Process design and economics
Connect biology to process behavior
Biological tools can examine metabolic networks, theoretical constraints, sequences, parts, and 
candidate designs. Their outputs have different levels of maturity. A model's theoretical production 
bound is not a measured yield or proof that the organism can be operated economically.
Process tools should connect those constraints to time, operating mode, material balances, and 
reactor requirements. Fitting a model needs an identified dataset, an objective, assumptions, 
diagnostics, and an account of parameter uncertainty. Synthetic data, estimates, and measured data 
must remain distinct.
A good fit to one dataset does not establish reliable prediction outside those conditions. Several 
parameter combinations may explain the same observations. Postdoc should be able to recommend 
tests that distinguish competing explanations.
Candidate-generation tools can propose biological parts, organism designs, or process changes 
where suitable. Postdoc should compare them against the project's need, supporting evidence, 
expected value of testing, cost, and available physical capacity. Generating many candidates is useful 
only when it helps select work that can answer the question.
Include recovery and the rest of production
The useful product must often be separated, concentrated, stabilized, finished, and packaged. 
pureOS is the proposed home for connecting these recovery and finishing operations. The stream 
leaving one step must retain the composition and conditions needed by the next.
Models should expose recovery losses, utilities, waste, and important properties. A rule-based or 
model-assisted process proposal is a candidate to investigate. Its readiness depends on the evidence 
and the validity of its component models.
Facility interfaces, preparation, cleaning, scheduling, storage, and quality checks can determine 
whether the process works in practice. They should become part of the connected production account 
as the relevant capabilities develop.
Connect models to actual costs
Proforma should support economic comparisons while preserving the origin of each important input. A 
library cost correlation, a preliminary estimate, a supplier quotation, a purchase, and an observed 
operating cost are different records.
Economics should include the costs relevant to the decision: materials, energy, water, labor, 
equipment, maintenance, downtime, rejected output, packaging, distribution, and working capital 
where applicable. Currency, date, location, plant scale, and financial assumptions must be visible.
Early estimates should show which assumptions drive the outcome. As fabrication and operation 
supply real data, the economic model can be revised. A successful reference calculation helps test 
software; it does not prove that a factory will make money.

8 Open equipment and manufacturing knowledge
Connect the model to the machine
Equipment is part of the central workflow. A process requires physical capability: mixing, heating, 
cooling, separation, measurement, control, or other functions. The equipment design should identify 
those requirements and the assumptions behind them.
openFerment should connect established CAD, simulation, electronics, and control tools where 
suitable. It should preserve editable source files, useful exchange formats, versions, calculations, and 
design rationale. A rendered image can explain a design, but it does not replace the files needed to 
modify it. [3]
Record the equipment lifecycle
The platform should distinguish a design from an actual machine and from the work performed on that 
machine:
1. Requirements define the intended function, capacity, interfaces, and operating conditions.
2. Design records preserve editable mechanical, piping, electrical, and control information.
3. Fabrication records identify the unit actually built, including materials, substitutions, deviations, and 
inspections.
4. Installation and commissioning record site conditions, configuration, calibration, functional tests, 
and acceptance.
5. Operation links runs, performance, faults, cleaning, and maintenance to that particular unit.
6. Revision records explain improvements and the evidence that motivated them.
Several machines can share one design while differing in construction, condition, utilities, and 
performance. Every important run should identify both the procedure version and the actual 
equipment configuration.
Publish the knowledge needed to make and support it
Useful design information includes bills of materials, specifications, tolerances, joining methods, 
surface requirements, drawings, wiring, firmware, and control configuration. Useful fabrication 
knowledge includes tooling, jigs, assembly sequence, inspections, and acceptance methods.
Operating knowledge includes site utilities, commissioning, calibration, cleaning, maintenance, spare 
parts, common faults, repair methods, and training. Photographs, video, annotations, and operator 
explanations can preserve details that drawings alone miss. Documenting this experience should be 
part of the work.
Purchased components can remain dependencies. Record their specifications, interfaces, sources, 
alternatives, and relevant rights. Open hardware does not require manufacturing every motor, sensor, 
seal, or computer from first principles. The CERN hardware licence addresses available components 
explicitly. [4]

Show demonstrated capability at its actual scale
A design proposal, reviewed calculation, fabricated prototype, commissioned machine, and 
demonstrated production line are different achievements. The platform should show which has 
occurred, under which conditions, and with which supporting records.
Increasing the capacity of a line and reproducing it at another site are separate engineering jobs. A 
proposed change should identify which assumptions transfer and what needs recalculation or testing. 
A larger drawing does not establish industrial readiness.
The relevant evidence may include mixing, heat transfer, oxygen supply, cycle time, recovery, quality 
consistency, cleaning, reliability, utilities, labor, waste, and cost. The exact requirements depend on 
the process. Failures and local adaptations can be as useful to the commons as successful runs.
Open documentation does not certify a machine or authorize its use. Competent review, safe limits, 
testing, and applicable requirements remain responsibilities of the people and organizations doing the 
physical work.
9 Physical work and learning from results
Prepare the work before testing
A runbook should connect a procedure to its purpose, equipment, materials, required skills, 
predictions, and planned observations. The record needs the version actually used. If the team 
changes a method during a run, it should preserve the amendment and reason.
Important predictions should be recorded before the results are known. They may be points, ranges, 
bounds, or qualitative expectations. The record should identify which method, model version, or 
estimate made each prediction and under which conditions it applies.
Postdoc can propose a procedure and help prepare materials for review. The responsible people 
decide whether the work is appropriate and authorized. A generic project permission is not enough to 
bypass a lab's operating rules.
Capture what actually happened
Bench is the place for observations, measurements, samples, timing, and deviations. It should work 
for the people performing the task, including situations with gloves, limited connectivity, or 
interruptions.
Manual entry, file import, and instrument streams can coexist. Record units, time, instrument identity, 
calibration status, machine configuration, and the relevant method. Raw readings, operator 
confirmations, and later corrections should remain distinguishable. An observation outside a planned 
step can still be useful when its context is preserved.
AI can assist with interpretation or entry, but a generated value is not an instrument reading. 
Unconfirmed imported or interpreted data should remain identifiable until the appropriate checks are 
complete.
Offline capture needs explicit ownership, synchronization, and conflict handling. Losing connectivity 
must not silently lose observations or create two contradictory authoritative histories.

Compare predictions with the actual evidence
After the run, compare the saved predictions with the observed outcomes. Preserve misses, 
supported predictions, inconclusive results, deviations, and missing data. The judgment should use 
the relevant measurement uncertainty and the question actually tested.
A successful overall result does not validate every input. Compensating errors can produce a good 
final answer. A process output may constrain several parameters together without identifying any one 
of them.
Calibration should therefore be recorded at the level the evidence supports. This may be a particular 
outcome, a model family, a task type, or a parameter measured independently. The system should 
not claim a parameter's accuracy solely because a batch reached its predicted final output.
Build a visible track record
Postdoc's trust should come from performance records that users can examine. Keep the predictions, 
outcomes, relevant conditions, model versions, and evaluation methods. Distinguish performance on 
familiar conditions from performance on new ones.
The record should include failures, stopped work, and inconclusive tests where known. Counting only 
successful completed runs would exaggerate reliability. A method can improve while still being 
unsuitable for some conditions.
Learning can begin with better retrieval, corrected inputs, improved model selection, wider uncertainty 
where justified, and better experimental design. Fine tuning or reinforcement learning may help later, 
but only with suitable permitted data and evidence of benefit over simpler approaches.
Private projects do not automatically become training data. Decisions about training require their own 
permissions, evaluation, versioning, and recovery plan if a change worsens performance. No fixed 
number of experiments guarantees improvement.
10 The commons and public rights
Support private work and deliberate publication
An open platform can serve projects that are private, shared within a group, or public. A user should 
be able to choose the permitted visibility of their own work. Organizations may adopt stronger 
publication commitments through separate policies.
The commons contains work deliberately shared under usable terms: reviewed extractions, 
measurements, designs, software, methods, training material, and production experience. Sharing 
should preserve attribution, versions, evidence, and permissions. Public records need correction and 
review pathways, including room for attributed disagreement.
Access to a paper is not permission to republish its full text or use it for every purpose. Likewise, a 
project participant may not own an employer's invention or a supplier's confidential design. Rights in 
contributed material need to be recorded and resolved before release.
Match licences to the material
The founding direction favors public commercial reuse, reciprocal sharing where suitable, and no 
proprietary dual licensing of the common project. The final package needs review across several 
kinds of material.

corresponding source, including specified network-use situations. It does not automatically require 
publication of every experiment or output produced with the software. [5]
ODbL version 1 is a database candidate. Rights in the database and rights in its individual contents 
require separate attention. The licence does not supply missing permission to redistribute a paper or 
other protected material. [6]
CERN OHL version 2 strongly reciprocal is a hardware candidate. Equipment source and products 
need terms suited to hardware. Documentation and teaching material also need appropriate content 
terms. [4]
Copyright, patent rights, contracts, confidentiality, and trademarks perform different jobs. In the United 
States, for example, copyright in an explanation does not itself grant exclusive control over the 
method it describes. Publishing a process under a document licence does not create a universal legal 
requirement that every user publish all improvements. Reciprocity must be stated within the rights and 
legal tools that can actually support it. [7]
A single label such as Bioprocess Reciprocity Licence should not be treated as a finished solution to 
all of these questions. Contribution agreements, enforcement authority, compatibility, and an explicit 
patent policy need deliberate design. Owning rights can help grant and defend public permissions. 
The promise against proprietary dual licensing also needs governance; a reciprocal licence alone 
does not prevent a rights owner offering separate terms over work it controls.
Publish useful work without claiming automatic patent clearance
The platform can help prepare reports and technical disclosures that explain methods, designs, 
evidence, and limits. Sources, simulations, estimates, and proposed work can be useful when 
accurately described. Untested work must not be represented as a demonstrated result.
A dated, accessible, sufficiently informative disclosure may become relevant prior art. Its legal effect 
depends on its content, timing, and applicable law. A DOI identifies a publication; it does not by itself 
establish that effect. Publication does not automatically cancel an existing patent or give permission to 
practice it. [8]
An optional strategic workflow can connect claim analysis, research selection, candidate tests, 
publication, and a dated rights landscape. The project should compare research value and legal 
relevance with the cost and limits of the work. AI claim classification is an aid to review, not a 
determination of validity, infringement, or freedom to operate.
Release records should identify the exact version, authorship, rights, reviewer, and authority to 
publish. Signatures and timestamps identify acts and versions. They do not certify scientific truth, 
safety, or legal clearance. Later corrections should link back to the original release.

12 Technical architecture
Build a shared record with connected tools
The architecture should have a common data model for identity, versions, origins, permissions, costs, 
tasks, and relationships. Domain modules then provide the scientific and engineering tools. This 
keeps the system extensible without requiring one giant model of every industry at the beginning.
Postdoc is the conversation and coordination layer. A task system supports delegation, budgets, 
cancellation, retries, and progress. Tool adapters provide typed inputs and identifiable results. A 
durable record holds the authoritative work. Search and indexes help find relevant records within the 
user's permissions.
External interfaces and exports should allow other tools and self-hosted installations to participate. 
Users need understandable formats and enough context to move their work. The public project should 
avoid making one hosted service the only usable copy of its own technical knowledge.
Keep authorization outside model persuasion
The application should enforce who may read, calculate, change records, spend, publish, and control 
equipment. A model's instruction to itself is not an access-control system. Authorization should be 
checked where the action occurs.
Important actions need records of the requester, agent or tool, relevant inputs, permission, result, and 
cost. Physical control needs separately engineered interfaces and safeguards appropriate to the 
equipment. General conversation should not directly become unrestricted machine control.
Make persistence and operation practical
Structured records, large files, indexes, and background tasks have different storage needs. The 
design must address backups, recovery, migrations, offline work, concurrency, and project isolation. 
SQLite may suit some early or local deployments; a hosted database may suit others. The choice 
should follow tested workflows.

Refactoring should preserve a reproducible project snapshot, tests of important behavior, and 
migration paths for existing data. Splitting large files is useful when it clarifies responsibilities. It is not 
evidence that a scientific workflow has become correct.
The first implementation should use the smallest architecture that supports one complete, trustworthy 
workflow. It should still leave room for multiple providers, permission-aware retrieval, equipment 
records, and shared measurement return.
Use API inference with controlled evidence acquisition
For the first flagship, Postdoc uses a hosted model API. This does not grant it independent web-
search authority. Its default scientific context is a finite, curated and versioned collection of sources 
and structured records. Workers use installed tools and the evidence made available to the task; they 
do not independently browse or install software at runtime.
A user may request a particular source or a bounded acquisition task through a controlled import path. 
Record what was requested, retrieved, reviewed and added. New material enters a new evidence 
version rather than silently changing an ongoing calculation or benchmark. Market information can 
arrive as a dated imported package under the same rule. The default can be revised by an explicit 
project decision; it is not a mandate to build an autonomous research agent.
Hosting a model is distinct from browsing. Approved inference sends selected context to the provider 
under the project's permissions and budget. The deployment is therefore not an entirely disconnected 
local system, and there is no requirement that the strongest model fit on the founder's computer. Tool 
and network restrictions need application enforcement. Retrieved content remains data to analyze, 
never authority to change those restrictions.

14 Stewardship and operating costs
openFerment needs a continuing party able to hold entrusted rights and domains, enter contracts, 
manage funds, pay maintainers, and arrange legal support. An independent nonprofit is the proposed 
structure. An existing fiscal sponsor could also be evaluated. Established project stewards provide 
examples of these services. [9]
The legal home, governing powers, asset transfers, contribution agreements, and public 
accountability remain to be decided. The steward must serve the public project rather than grant 
exclusive privileges to a founder, business, or sponsor. A nonprofit does not create immunity or 
supply a legal budget merely by existing.
Open-source software still costs money to maintain. Development, shared storage and indexes, 
security, documentation, administration, and legal work need funding. Recurring supporters, suitable 
grants, sponsorship, paid hosting, support, and commissioned public development are possible 
sources.
Shared project costs and a user's model work should be accounted for separately. User-supplied 
keys, local models, or metered services can fund particular tasks. Free access must not imply 
unlimited compute. Limits, prices where charged, and who pays should be clear before work is 
authorized.
Open licences do not automatically make every user donate or publish their results. Sustainability 
needs explicit funding arrangements. Long-term independence also benefits from multiple funders 
and the ability to export and self-host the work.

15 Success and open decisions
Measure useful work and honest reliability
Success begins with investigations that answer real questions and preserve enough information for 
another person to inspect them. Measure whether users can recover sources, understand estimates, 
repeat calculations, capture physical work, and explain why a decision changed.
For Postdoc and routing, measure task quality, calibration where applicable, total cost, latency, failed 
work, and the value of review. For equipment and processes, measure demonstrated performance, 
quality, reliability, resource use, actual cost, and reproducibility under stated conditions.
For the commons, measure useful reuse, corrections, maintained designs, and replication by other 
teams. Downloads, agent counts, citations, and confident prose are incomplete measures of practical 
value.
Maintain decisions with their reasons
The platform needs a maintained decision register. Each important choice should identify its owner, 
evidence, alternatives, reason, and review condition. An earlier design note should not become 
binding just because it has been repeated.
The immediate decisions include the first complete demonstration, the shared data model, 
measurement return, origins and uncertainty rules, initial routing evaluations, permission boundaries, 
the first equipment records, publication terms, and a sustainable operating arrangement.
Detailed licence and patent policies, the nonprofit's legal form, model-training permissions, and future 
physical-control integrations also remain open. These choices should be resolved in the order 
required by actual work. The purpose stays clear: help people understand and improve productive 
systems, and make the knowledge they choose to share genuinely usable by others.
16 The cellulosic ethanol flagship
Define the first problem narrowly
The initial question is how a characterized biomass can be converted into ethanol through the 
supported process family, under declared technical and economic conditions. The first case should 
use one documented corn-stover pairing. Switchgrass is a later comparison when its evidence is 
suitable. Similar composition does not establish the same pretreatment response, digestibility, 
inhibitor behavior, or strain performance.
The confirmed operating boundary is batch biological conversion; downstream recovery may use 
another declared mode. The reference-case brief names which hydrolysis and fermentation stages 
form the batch block. Each stage, including pretreatment and stages outside that block, states its 
actual mode, inputs, outputs and timing. Do not relabel a historical mixed-mode reference as an 
entirely batch plant. Coupling batch discharge to continuous recovery can require buffers, feasible 
throughput and utility assumptions. A fixed supported schedule is a useful first step; a universal 
optimized scheduler is not required at the beginning.
BioSTEAM and its associated biorefinery models are foundations to inspect and reuse. They already 
provide process modeling and batch-design support. The historical NREL corn-stover design provides 
a separate reference for process and economic assumptions. Reproducing a pinned software

example, reconciling it with a historical design, and validating predictions against independent 
observations are three different achievements. [11][12]
The BioSTEAM comparison uses corn stover with dilute acid and switchgrass with AFEX. It does not 
establish calibrated performance for every crossed combination. Its example uncertainty assumptions 
are explicitly illustrative. New feedstock or process combinations need evidence rather than 
permission inferred from a software selector. [11]
Keep a useful feedstock and evidence record
Record material lot and sample, composition and measurement basis, moisture, solids, relevant 
physical properties, analytical methods, important contaminants, conversion behavior, available 
quantity, delivered cost and operating context. Unknown properties remain unknown. A material 
described only by carbohydrate fractions may support a conditional balance while remaining 
unsuitable for predictive process ranking.
Dry, as-received and other analytical bases must not be mixed silently. Preserve unresolved 
composition and assess closure rather than normalizing an incomplete assay into apparent 
completeness. Laboratory analytical procedures provide a basis for describing what was measured; 
they do not by themselves predict conversion performance. [13]
The curated collection should connect numerical claims to actual source locations, methods, 
conditions and rights. A folder of PDFs is not enough for dependable calculations. Relevant new 
methods may enter a research catalogue; only implemented methods with stated applicability and 
checked behavior become executable candidates. There is no promise that the first version contains 
every contemporary pretreatment.
Separate supported decisions from favorable assumptions
Candidate generation starts with a small set of compatible installed operations. Optimization can 
change a controllable condition only where a supported relation connects that condition to 
performance. Selecting a higher hydrolysis yield or lower inhibitor concentration without such a 
relation is a scenario assumption, not an engineered improvement.
Inhibitors, washing and detoxification require condition-specific evidence. Removing an impurity may 
also remove sugar, consume water or add waste and cost. Unknown interactions should appear as 
limitations, alternative explanations or research hypotheses. The model must not invent universal 
tolerance thresholds to complete a flowsheet.
Compare checked candidates within their stated domains, conditional extrapolations, and unevaluated 
hypotheses separately. A useful conclusion can be that candidates are indistinguishable under the 
available evidence, that no candidate is feasible, or that characterization is insufficient. Report the 
best verified candidate among searched alternatives; claim a global optimum only when justified.
Use economic objectives at the right boundary
Begin with per-batch output, recovery assumptions, cycle timing and a clearly named partial cost 
where that is all the model covers. Minimum ethanol selling price becomes a meaningful plant-level 
comparison only with a consistent production, recovery, purity, capital, utilities, waste, financing, 
currency and economic-year boundary. It remains a conditional estimate, not a quotation or 
demonstrated commercial viability.

recovery or wastewater costs must not silently become zero. Historical cost years and reference

An ethanol module also cannot determine the globally best use of a biomass resource. If asked what

17 Test whether specialization earns its cost
State the hypothesis before claiming the advantage
The research hypothesis is that reusable scientific tools, structured evidence and appropriate 
coordination help an equally capable AI produce more dependable engineering decisions with less 
measured work on a defined class of tasks. It may prove true for some jobs and false for others. A 
specialist role using different instructions is not automatically a separately trained model.
Begin with a competent general agent allowed to use the same underlying scientific libraries, 
equations and source evidence. Compare it with the minimal openFerment workflow. A tested 
wrapper, structured record or curated extraction may be the intervention; state which one changed. 
Do not simultaneously give one side better data, a stronger model, additional permissions and a 
larger budget.
If the underlying models or their versions differ, report a practical system comparison and its 
confounding factors, not an isolated architectural effect. Only add a specialist comparison when the 
additional role exists and addresses a real failure. Compare it with the simpler path. Two nominal 
experimental arms that behave identically should be merged. The outcome can favor a smaller 
architecture. Cost-aware baselines and evaluating behavior across resource limits are supported by 
existing agent-evaluation methodology, but do not establish an openFerment advantage. [14][15]
Make a small fair comparison first
Fix the question, acceptable result, source snapshot, numerical boundary, model/version, tools, 
network authority, aggregate usage limit, simulation limit and retry policy before looking at results. 
Disable independent web research for both sides. Count all specialist calls, checks, retries and 
answer selection against the same total allowance. Preserve failures and budget-exhausted attempts.

openFerment  |  22
Use a small starter pack that covers a reference calculation, a supported comparison, incomplete data 
and a nearby unsupported case. Keep development examples separate from reserved variations. 
Choose repetitions after seeing pilot variability and cost; do not make the pasted hundred-case 
experiment or arbitrary improvement percentages requirements. A first-month diagnostic pilot 
supports development decisions, not sweeping statistical claims.
Measure validity and useful outcomes
Check units, balances, applicability, required inputs, operating modes and economic boundaries 
before rewarding objective value or prose. Use independently derived reference answers where 
possible and identify unresolved domain review. A matching simulation is not experimental validation. 
A best-known reference is not necessarily a proven optimum.
Assess creativity separately: distinct hypotheses, plausible mechanisms, relevance and discriminating 
tests. Lack of a matching local citation does not prove worldwide novelty. LLM agreement or a model's 
verbal confidence cannot serve as the sole judge of scientific correctness.
Report observable resources and full costs
Record provider-reported usage, model calls, retries, solver evaluations, local simulation time and 
memory where feasible, elapsed time, cost under a dated schedule, and human review. Report 
unsuccessful and incomplete work alongside accepted results. Costs per verified success need an 
explicit denominator; if nothing succeeds, that ratio is undefined.
Tokens, CPU time, price and energy are different measurements. Hosted GPU energy is generally not 
available to this project. Do not infer an energy saving from a token count or label subscription price 
as per-task compute. Compare quality at the same observable resource budget, or the resources 
needed to reach comparable verified quality. Broader claims require further evidence.
Account separately for development, data curation, maintenance and precomputation. State whether 
runs begin with existing memory or warm caches, give comparable opportunities where relevant, and 
prevent evaluation answers leaking into shared state. A reusable library can save repeated work while 
still having a substantial construction cost.
A result may show better quality at lower observed cost, similar quality at lower cost, better records at 
higher cost, task-dependent tradeoffs, or no demonstrated advantage. April should hand over an 
honest comparison pack and its limitations. Architecture should follow that evidence rather than a 
predetermined need for many agents.
